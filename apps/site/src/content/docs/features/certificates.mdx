Caddy obtains and renews certificates on its own. CPM's job is to tell it what to obtain, to show
you what exists, and to hold the credentials that DNS challenges need.

## Automatic

Every proxy host gets HTTPS by default through ACME - Let's Encrypt, ZeroSSL, or any directory you
point it at. The Certificates page shows issuer, expiry and status for each one.

For an internal CA, set the ACME directory URL under **Settings → ACME Server** and supply its root
so Caddy trusts it.

## DNS-01

HTTP-01 needs port 80 reachable from the internet. DNS-01 does not, which is what makes wildcard
certificates and internal-only hosts possible.

Twenty providers are supported, including Cloudflare, Route 53, DigitalOcean, Hetzner, Vultr,
Porkbun, GoDaddy, Namecheap, OVH, IONOS, Linode, netcup, deSEC, Dynu, acme-dns and ClouDNS.
Credentials are encrypted at rest, and propagation delay and timeout are configurable per provider
- netcup ships with slow-propagation defaults because it needs them.

A provider can be overridden per certificate when one domain lives somewhere else.

:::note
A DNS provider is a compiled-in Caddy module. If the one you want is greyed out, turn it on in
[Caddy Build](../caddy-build/) and rebuild.
:::

## Manual imports

Bring your own certificate and key. Expiry is tracked and surfaced the same way managed ones are,
so an import that is about to lapse is visible rather than a surprise.

## The built-in CA

CPM can act as its own certificate authority for client certificates - see
[access lists & mTLS](../access-control/). Issued certificates are tracked and revocable, and
revocation is fail-closed: revoking every certificate rejects every connection rather than falling
open.
